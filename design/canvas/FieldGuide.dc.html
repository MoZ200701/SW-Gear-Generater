Gear A · Gear B — one card, shown for the field with the caret
Module · mTooth size, in mm: the pitch diameter divided by the number of teeth. A bigger module means bigger, stronger teeth and fewer of them for the same diameter. Two gears mesh only if their modules match.here m 1.5 × z 20 = PD 30 mm · tooth depth 3.375 · circular pitch 4.712
Teeth · zHow many teeth go round the gear. With the module it sets the size, PD = m × z, and the ratio between two gears is the ratio of their tooth counts. Fewer than 17 undercuts the root at 20°.here z 20 → PD 30 mm · OD 33 mm · no undercut (17 or more)
Pressure angle · αThe slant of the flank: the angle between the line along which the teeth push on each other and the tangent to the pitch circle. 20° is the standard; 14.5° runs quieter but undercuts sooner, 25° is stronger but loads the bearings more. Meshing gears must share it.here 20° → base diameter 28.191 mm · undercut below 17 teeth
Face width · bHow thick the gear is along its axis: the depth it is extruded. Wider carries more load; 6 to 12 × module is usual for a spur gear.here 10 mm = 6.7 × module
BoreThe hole through the centre for the shaft. Blank makes a solid disc. Leave a rim between the bore and the root circle: the form warns under one module, and two to three is usual.here 10 mm → rim 8.125 mm to the root circle
Backlash · jHow much each tooth is thinned at the pitch circle so the pair does not bind: the space grows by this and the tooth shrinks by it, on both gears, so the play in the mesh is twice the number. Blank uses 0.3 × module, the default the equations carry; ISO asks 0.02–0.05 × m for a precise pair.here blank → 0.45 mm (0.3 m) · tooth 1.906 · space 2.806 · play in the mesh 0.9
Fillet · ρThe radius where each flank meets the root circle. It rounds off the sharp inside corner where a tooth would otherwise crack. Blank uses 0.38 × module.here blank → 0.57 mm (0.38 m)
Profile shift · xHow far the cutting rack is moved out from the gear, in modules. A positive shift makes the tip and root circles bigger and the tooth thicker, which cures undercut on a small pinion; the shifts of a pair together move its centre distance, which is how a standard module is made to fit one. 0 is the standard tooth.here x +0.12 → OD 34.48 mm · root 25.48 · tooth at pitch 2.716 · undercut below 14.96 teeth
Helix angle · βHow far the teeth lean from the axis, measured on the pitch cylinder. Module and pressure angle are then the normal ones, square to the teeth; round the gear the pitch is longer, mt = m / cos β. Steeper runs smoother but pushes the gear harder along its shaft: 8–25° is usual for a helical gear, 20–40° for a herringbone.here β 15° → mt 1.5529 · PD 31.058 mm · lead 364.15 · overlap εβ 1.098
HandWhich way the teeth wind: a right-hand gear's lean like a right-hand screw thread. Helical gears on parallel shafts mesh only with opposite hands, and a pinion inside a ring with the same hand; in a pair gear B's hand follows gear A's. A herringbone's names its half nearest the Front plane.here right → wound like a right-hand thread, 19.77° round across the face
Groove · gA plain channel round the middle of a herringbone gear, between its two sets of teeth, where a hob or a grinding wheel runs out. Its floor is two modules inside the root circle. Blank leaves the two halves meeting at a point, as they do on a gear cut in one piece.here 8 mm → 16 mm of teeth each side · floor ⌀ 37.188 mm
TeethWhich side of the rim the teeth are cut on. External teeth point outward, as on any gear; internal teeth point inward from a ring, and a pinion runs inside it turning the same way. A bevel gear's side is set by its cone, not here: outside a pitch cone under 90°, inside one past it.here internal → teeth point inward from a 137 mm rim, tips at 116 mm
RimA ring's outside diameter: the solid rim round the roots of its teeth. Blank leaves three modules of rim outside the root circle; under two modules the ring is thin.here blank → 137 mm → 6 mm of rim outside the root circle
Shaft angle · ΣThe angle between the two shafts a bevel gear works across, 90° for a right-angle drive. Each gear's pitch cone is its share of it, set by the two tooth counts; both gears of a pair must be cut for the same angle. Past 90° the bigger gear's cone opens out: where it reaches 90° exactly it is a crown gear, its teeth on its face, and beyond that an internal bevel gear, its teeth inside a cone.here Σ 90° → this gear's cone 36.87°, its mate's 53.13°
Mate teeth · z₂How many teeth the gear this bevel gear meshes with has. A bevel gear's cone, and the Gleason split of its tooth depth, depend on its mate; in a pair it is the other gear's teeth.here z₂ 40 → cone distance 50 mm · addendum 2.402 · dedendum 1.974
NameThe file the part is saved as, without .SLDPRT. Blank uses the name shown, built from module and teeth; in a pair the smaller gear is the pinion.here Gear_m1.5_N20.SLDPRT in D:\Gears
Pair — the same card under the solver's fields
Centre distance · CShaft to shaft, centre to centre. Standard gears sit at C = m (z₁ + z₂) / 2, their pitch circles touching, so C fixes how many teeth the pair has between them once a module is chosen.here 60 mm → z₁ + z₂ = 80 at m 1.5 · 96 at m 1.25 · 120 at m 1
Cone distance · RFrom the pitch apex, where the two bevel gears' axes meet, out along the pitch cone to the back of the teeth. It is a bevel pair's size, as the centre distance is a spur pair's: R = m √(z₁² + z₂² + 2 z₁ z₂ cos Σ) / (2 sin Σ).here 50 mm at Σ 90° → m 2 × z 30 gives R 50
Ratio · iTurns of the driver for one turn of the driven gear, z₂ / z₁. 3 or 3:1 makes the second gear three times the first; 1:3 makes it a third.here 3 → 20 : 60
Module · mPin the module the solver may use, or leave it blank to try every standard one in the chosen series.here blank → 1 · 1.25 · 1.5 · 2 · 2.5 · 3 · 4 … from Series 1
SeriesWhich standard modules the solver tries. Series 1 is the preferred set (1, 1.25, 1.5, 2, 2.5 …); 1 + 2 adds the in-between sizes (1.125, 1.375 …); + fine adds 0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8 and 0.9 for small gears.here Series 1 → 18 modules from 1 to 50 (ISO 54)
Teeth · min to maxThe tooth counts the solver may give either gear. Below 17 a 20° pinion undercuts; the maximum keeps the list short.here 17 to 200 → 69 candidates at C 60, i 3 · 3 exact · 2 with shift
MeshExternal puts the two gears side by side, turning opposite ways. Internal makes gear B a ring with gear A inside it, turning the same way, at C = m (z₂ − z₁) / 2.here internal → gear B is a ring round gear A, C = m (z₂ − z₁) / 2
ShaftsParallel shafts mesh helical gears of equal helix and opposite hand. Crossed shafts mesh them at an angle: of the same hand their two helix angles add up to the shaft angle, of opposite hands they differ by it.here crossed → |β₁ − β₂| = 30°, opposite hands
Shaft angle · ΣThe angle between the two shafts. A crossed helical pair splits it between its helix angles; a bevel pair between its pitch cones.here Σ 30°
HandsWhether the two gears of a crossed pair are the same hand or opposite hands. The same hand splits the shaft angle between the two helices, β₁ + β₂ = Σ, and suits a large angle such as 90°; opposite hands make it the difference, β₁ − β₂ = Σ, which reaches a small angle with two steep helices.here opposite → β₁ − β₂ = ±30°, gear B the other hand from gear A
AssemblyFixed holds both gears still, meshed: gear 1 fixed and gear 2 mated where its teeth meet gear 1's. Gear mate lets gear 1 turn on its axis and adds a SolidWorks gear mate, so dragging gear 1 turns gear 2 at the tooth ratio, the other way round, or the same way round a ring.here fixed → gear 1 fixed, gear 2 mated at the mesh angle; nothing turns
Assembly nameThe file the assembly is saved as, without .SLDASM. Blank uses the name shown, built from the module and both tooth counts.here Pair_m1.5_20T_60T.SLDASM in D:\Gears
Each card is worked out for a gear that has the field: the spur pair of the main artboard (m 1.5, 20 : 60 at C 60) unless it belongs to a shifted, helical, grooved, ring, bevel or crossed one.

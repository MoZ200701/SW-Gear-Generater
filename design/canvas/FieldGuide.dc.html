Gear A · Gear B — one card, shown for the field with the caret
Module · mTooth size, in mm: the pitch diameter divided by the number of teeth. A bigger module means bigger, stronger teeth and fewer of them for the same diameter. Two gears mesh only if their modules match.here m 1.5 × z 20 = PD 30 mm · tooth depth 3.375 · circular pitch 4.712
Teeth · zHow many teeth go round the gear. With the module it sets the size, PD = m × z, and the ratio between two gears is the ratio of their tooth counts. Fewer than 17 undercuts the root at 20°.here z 20 → PD 30 mm · OD 33 mm · no undercut (17 or more)
Pressure angle · αThe slant of the flank: the angle between the line along which the teeth push on each other and the tangent to the pitch circle. 20° is the standard; 14.5° runs quieter but undercuts sooner, 25° is stronger but loads the bearings more. Meshing gears must share it.here 20° → base diameter 28.191 mm · undercut below 17 teeth
Face width · bHow thick the gear is along its axis: the depth it is extruded. Wider carries more load; 6 to 12 × module is usual for a spur gear.here 10 mm = 6.7 × module
BoreThe hole through the centre for the shaft. Blank makes a solid disc. Leave a rim between the bore and the root circle: the form warns under one module, and two to three is usual.here 10 mm → rim 8.125 mm to the root circle
Backlash · jHow much each tooth is thinned at the pitch circle so the pair does not bind: the space grows by this and the tooth shrinks by it, on both gears, so the play in the mesh is twice the number. Blank uses 0.3 × module, the default the equations carry; ISO asks 0.02–0.05 × m for a precise pair.here blank → 0.45 mm (0.3 m) · tooth 1.906 · space 2.806 · play in the mesh 0.9
Fillet · ρThe radius where each flank meets the root circle. It rounds off the sharp inside corner where a tooth would otherwise crack. Blank uses 0.38 × module.here blank → 0.57 mm (0.38 m)
NameThe file the part is saved as, without .SLDPRT. Blank uses the name shown, built from module and teeth; in a pair the smaller gear is the pinion.here Pinion_m1.5_N20.SLDPRT in D:\Gears
Pair — the same card under the solver's fields
Centre distance · CShaft to shaft, centre to centre. Standard gears sit at C = m (z₁ + z₂) / 2, their pitch circles touching, so C fixes how many teeth the pair has between them once a module is chosen.here 60 mm → z₁ + z₂ = 80 at m 1.5 · 96 at m 1.25 · 120 at m 1
Ratio · iTurns of the driver for one turn of the driven gear, z₂ / z₁. 3 or 3:1 makes the second gear three times the first; 1:3 makes it a third.here 3 → 20 : 60
Module · mPin the module the solver may use, or leave it blank to try every standard one in the chosen series.here blank → 1 · 1.25 · 1.5 · 2 · 2.5 · 3 · 4 … from Series 1
SeriesWhich standard modules the solver tries. Series 1 is the preferred set (1, 1.25, 1.5, 2, 2.5 …); 1 + 2 adds the in-between sizes (1.125, 1.375 …); + fine adds 0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8 and 0.9 for small gears.here Series 1 → 18 modules from 1 to 50 (ISO 54)
Teeth · min to maxThe tooth counts the solver may give either gear. Below 17 a 20° pinion undercuts; the maximum keeps the list short.here 17 to 200 → 64 candidates at C 60, i 3 · 3 exact
Assembly nameThe file the assembly is saved as, without .SLDASM. Blank uses the name shown, built from the module and both tooth counts.here Pair_m1.5_20T_60T.SLDASM in D:\Gears
